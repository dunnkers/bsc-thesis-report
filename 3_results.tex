\section{Results}\label{sec:results}
The data-set was tested under several configuration conditions. Different- pixels size configurations and classifiers were used, all using a $10$-fold cross validation setup. The study focused on two classifiers: a \textit{Support Vector Machine} using LibSVM \citep{chang2011libsvm} and \textit{Gradient Boosting} using XGBoost \citep{chen2016xgboost}. General results of the prediction are first explored, using the \textit{XGBoost} classifier. Afterward a comparison between the two classifiers is made.

\subsection{Classifier comparison}
% Plot a grouped boxplot with SVM/XGBoost compared.
Predictions of different accuracy levels were explored, all for the XGBoost classifier. Classifier performance is similar for most situations, therefore all individual metrics for the SVM classifier are not explored. Instead a comparison is drawn of the performance of the two classifiers. A box-plot categorical comparison is drawn, where every box-plot was supplied with the means of their respective folds, which means in the case of $n\_splits = 10$, there will be 10 data-points per box-plot. See Figure~\ref{fig:classifiers-comparison}.

% Note we are missing 1 metric for SVM. Explain.
Important to note is the fact that for the $525$x$1050$ cache, results are only obtained for XGBoost. This is due to the very high testing times of the SVM classifier when large pixel size configurations come into play. Although training times for both classifiers remain somewhat constant despite increasing pixel sizes, testing times per image get significantly higher with higher pixel size configurations. The reason for this is that the training set is sampled, reducing the amount of pixels that are used for training, which applies to all pixel configurations. A max amount of samples of $max\_samples$ are taken per image. In testing, however, an increase in pixel size means a significant increase in total data-points that have to be predicted. Classifier- and pipeline CPU performance is further explored in Section~\ref{sec:pipeline-performance}.

\begin{figure}[ht]
  \centering
  \includesvg[width=\linewidth]{img/max_samples=200,folds=10-classifiers-performance}
  \caption{Classifiers performance comparison, taken using the means for every split, for $n\_splits = 10$; resulting in 10 data-points per bar.
  }\label{fig:classifiers-comparison}
\end{figure}

% Confusion matrix.
In order to further explore the statistical difference in also the error made by both classifiers, a confusion-matrix is used, drawing a comparison of true labeled classes vs. expected labels. All output images of the designated cache and all its according groundtruth images are taken into consideration. Every predicted data-point is then compared to its true value - in the case of the $175$x$350$ cache $61.250,000$ million data-points are compared. XGBoost- and SVM confusion matrices are presented in Figures- \ref{fig:xgboost-confusion-matrix} and \ref{fig:svm-confusion-matrix} respectively.

\begin{figure}[ht]
  \centering
  \includesvg[width=\linewidth]{img/max_samples=200,folds=10,clf=XGBoost-confusion-matrix}
  \caption{XGBoost confusion matrix for the $175$x$350$ cache.
  }\label{fig:xgboost-confusion-matrix}
\end{figure}

\begin{figure}[ht]
  \centering
  \includesvg[width=\linewidth]{img/max_samples=200,folds=10,clf=SVM-confusion-matrix}
  \caption{SVM confusion matrix for the $175$x$350$ cache.
  }\label{fig:svm-confusion-matrix}
\end{figure}

Shown by the confusion matrices is that XGBoost classifies more data-points as non- road markings than the SVM classifier, even though it is actually a road-marker data-point. This in comparison to the prediction of the road-marker label, which the SVM classifier predicted wrongly more often, $0.0598$ scoring vs. $0.0575$ scoring. In overall both classifiers achieve an accuracy score of approximately $0.94$ for the $175$x$350$ cache.
